01

Colour

One dark room, four lights. Each app is a colour, and the page takes that colour when you're inside it.

  • One product colour per screen. Shared pages (home, points, sign in) stay ink, with only the eyes in colour.
  • Black text on every colour. Ink on yellow or lime can't be read.
  • Lines, not boxes. Hairlines separate things. A surface is for something you hold, like a ticket or a sheet.
  • Red does two jobs only: the laser dot, and stop.

02

Type

Three faces with three jobs. Anybody shouts, the text face talks, Martian Mono labels.

  • Three scales, not one fluid scale. Each device gets its own sizes, so tablet is never a stretched phone.
  • Anybody is for display only. It narrows on smaller screens so long words fit: hero is width 100 on phone, 112 on tablet, 118 on desktop. It goes past 125 only for a moment, like Stretch.
  • Mono is always uppercase and never a sentence. Dates, counts, codes, labels.
  • Line length: body up to 64 characters, lead up to 40.
  • Nothing under 11px. Nothing in mono under 11px either.

The text face: Instrument Sans

Picked 12 Nov
Doors at 9. Kohra plays at 1. Say you're going and you'll see who else is. Your ticket shows up here and at the door. Why it won. Tighter and more editorial than Outfit, with a clear a, e and g at 14px in dense screens like the guest list. It keeps out of Anybody's way, so the display face and the objects carry the fun.

03

Spacing

Ten steps, one scale, everywhere. This is what makes a page feel compact and still breathe.

  • Inside a component: s1 to s3. Label to its content: s3.
  • Parts of one component: s4. Components side by side: the gutter.
  • Components in a section, and a chapter head to its body: s6. An extra block in a chapter: s7.
  • Between sections: the section value, 48 on phone, 72 on tablet, 96 on desktop. A chapter gets half above and half below, so two chapters sit one section apart.
  • One container: 1240 wide plus margins. Laptops from 1100 to 1536 use the desktop grid; only the hero type grows with the screen.
  • No in-between values. If 24 is too much and 16 too little, the layout is wrong, not the scale.

07

Layers

Eleven fixed layers. Nothing makes up its own. The cat is always on top, because it's a cat.

  • The header always has a backing once anything can scroll under it: surface 1 at 82%, blurred, with a hairline.
  • Only one thing floats over content at a time: a sheet or a prompt, never both.
  • The object sits under the header and over the page. It never covers text.

08

Motion

Six speeds, four curves. Things arrive fast and settle soft, like a cat landing.

  • Motion follows the music. When a track plays, loops lock to its tempo. Nothing else pulses on its own.
  • Spring is a treat: pounces, stamps landing, the object flipping. Not every hover.
  • Sound never starts by itself. It's offered after a little scrolling, and only plays after a tap.
  • Reduced motion keeps the meaning and drops the travel.

09

The object

The logo as a coin. It is the page's player: it sits in the home hero, and everywhere else it lives in the player pill at the bottom. Every section also has its own chrome object, from the registry below.

  • The coin stays a coin. Tap it to play the page's track, grab it to scratch the track.
  • One chrome object per meaning. Nights is the mirror ball, Host the wristband, Artists the record, School the dial, Gear the speaker, Episodes the cassette, Points the stamp. Nothing is used for two things.
  • Sizes: a chapter head 120 / 144 / 168, a flow card 64 then 96 on desktop, an app tile 64 then 96.
  • They move: turn on their own, spin with scroll, tilt toward the pointer, jump on the kick, and a tap sends one round with a chirp. With less motion they only follow the pointer and scroll.

10

Words

Write like someone who's been to a thousand of these nights and still gets excited about the next one.

  • Say what happens, not how the site works.
  • No slogans, no em dashes, no exclamation marks unless a person would shout it.
  • Buttons are verbs you'd say out loud: I'm going, Book, Send it.
  • Real names and real places. If a number is an example, the page says so.
  • Nothing about founders. No Bollywood.

Opus · Handoff

Handoff

What the real build needs: stack, components and props, data, budgets, assets, and what's still open.

Opus is the Cats Can Dance design system: one set of tokens, 38 components, and 25 page templates, each working on phone, tablet and desktop. This is what the real build needs. The lab is the reference for every behaviour, and its source is in the project under ccd/design-system/.

1. What to build with

  • Next.js App Router, in the CCD repo (paramminhas5/CatsCanDanceOct2026), deployed on Vercel. Supabase stays the one database.
  • Tokens as CSS variables. Ship tokens.css as generated, and generate it from tokens.json in CI with the same build.py, so nobody hand-edits a value. If the repo uses Tailwind, map the theme to the variables (spacing: { s1: 'var(--s1)' … }, colors: { nights: 'var(--c-nights)' … }) and switch off Tailwind's default scale so off-scale classes don't exist.
  • The lint goes into CI. build.py fails on any raw padding, margin, gap, inset, font-size, radius, z-index or duration. Run it on every CSS module and on inline styles in JSX.
  • Fonts self-hosted with next/font/local: Anybody (variable, wdth 50 to 150), Instrument Sans (variable), Martian Mono. font-display: swap, and preload Anybody and Instrument Sans.
  • No animation library needed. Every movement in Opus is CSS transitions and keyframes driven by classes and custom properties, plus requestAnimationFrame for the eyes, the laser cat and the scratch record. Keep it that way; it's why everything respects prefers-reduced-motion in one place.

4. The eyes engine

One loop at the app root, not one per button.

  • An IntersectionObserver tracks which eye elements are on screen. Only those update.
  • Each frame, every visible pair eases 18% toward its target and writes --px and --py on its container. The CSS turns those into pupil positions.
  • The target depends on the button's state: loading chases a figure-eight, focus looks straight out, empty looks down, the see-all looks at its arrow, the laser overrides everything.
  • Every 6 to 10 seconds the nearest pair pounces (every 1 to 2 with the laser on).
  • With no pointer for 2.5 seconds, or on touch, eyes look around slowly. After a tap they look at the tap for 1.8 seconds.
  • Under reduced motion: no pounce, no blink, no chase. Following stays.

6. Accessibility

  • AA contrast is checked on the foundations page. Product colours always carry near-black text. faint (#86867F) is the lowest grey for text. rest is never text.
  • Every control works by keyboard. Disabled buttons use aria-disabled and stay focusable so their reason is read out.
  • Live regions: button results, the door's answer, RSVP status, search results.
  • Hit areas are at least 48 even when a control looks smaller.
  • Reduced motion is one media query per component. It keeps meaning and drops travel.

7. Performance budgets

  • Largest Contentful Paint under 2.0 s on a mid-range Android over 4G. The hero text and the first poster are the LCP, never the WebGL object.
  • The object loads after first paint and only when on screen. One WebGL context per page.
  • Posters in WebP, sized to their slot, lazy below the fold. The torch wall uses thumbnails.
  • JS under 120 KB gzipped for a product page before the instrument loads. The instrument and the audio engine load when you first press Play.
  • The eyes loop only touches visible elements. The laser cat's loop stops when the cat is gone.

10. How to know it's done

  • Each page matches its three screens in the lab at 390, 820 and 1440, and looks right in between.
  • The token lint passes in CI.
  • Lighthouse accessibility 100 on every template; axe finds nothing.
  • The reduced-motion pass is tested on a real phone.
  • The door is tested at a real night with 40 people.
